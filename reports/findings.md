# Inventory Intelligence — Business Findings Report

## 1. Project Overview

Inventory Intelligence is a supply chain analytics project built using Python, Pandas, SQLite, SQL, and Power BI.

The objective is to analyze inventory, sales, estimated revenue, gross profit, and demand forecasts through a reproducible data-processing workflow and an interactive dashboard.

The analysis uses a simulated supply chain dataset containing 91,250 records covering January 1, 2024, through December 30, 2024.

**Financial metric note:** The dataset does not specify a currency. Revenue and gross profit are therefore reported in unspecified monetary units. Gross profit is calculated before operating expenses and should not be interpreted as net profit.

## 3. Business Findings

### Finding 1: Revenue differs across warehouses

Warehouse 2 recorded the highest estimated revenue at approximately 7.40 million, while Warehouse 1 recorded approximately 6.27 million.

This indicates differences in the estimated revenue generated by the warehouses during the observed period.

**Business relevance:** Warehouse-level revenue comparisons can help analysts investigate differences in product mix, sales volume, and pricing. Revenue alone does not establish which warehouse is most operationally efficient.

### Finding 2: A small portion of records were at or below the reorder point

Approximately 5.52% of the inventory records had inventory levels at or below the corresponding reorder point.

This represents a historical, record-level replenishment signal. It does not represent the percentage of unique products currently requiring replenishment.

**Business relevance:** These records could be examined further to understand when inventory approached replenishment thresholds. A practical replenishment decision would also require current stock levels, outstanding orders, lead times, and demand information.

### Finding 3: Revenue is concentrated differently across products

SKU_38 recorded the highest estimated revenue among the products analyzed, at approximately 1.06 million.

The Top 10 Products chart highlights products contributing the most estimated revenue in the dataset.

**Business relevance:** Product-level revenue analysis can help prioritize further investigation into sales contribution, profitability, and inventory planning. High revenue does not necessarily mean high profit margin or high inventory priority.

### Finding 4: Actual sales and demand forecasts follow a similar seasonal pattern

The Power BI time-series chart shows actual sales and demand forecasts moving closely together through the observed period. Both series rise around March–April and fall around September–October.

The numerical forecast evaluation produced:

- Mean Absolute Error (MAE): 2.38 units.
- Mean bias (forecast − actual sales): +0.03 units.
- Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE): 16.32%, calculated only for records with positive actual sales.

The MAPE calculation excluded 620 records with zero actual sales.

**Business relevance:** These metrics provide a starting point for evaluating the supplied forecasts. Further evaluation against a simple baseline and across different products or periods would be needed before concluding that the forecasts are sufficiently accurate for operational use.

### Finding 5: Python and SQL calculations were independently validated

Eight key metrics calculated using Pandas and SQLite matched during validation. These included total records, units sold, estimated revenue, estimated gross profit, average inventory, the percentage of records at or below reorder point, forecast MAE, and forecast bias.

**Business relevance:** Independent calculation provides confidence that the Python and SQL implementations are consistent for the tested metrics.

## 4. Data Quality and Validation

The dataset contained 91,250 records and 15 columns.

The completed data-quality checks found:

- No missing values.
- No duplicate rows.
- No duplicate SKU–warehouse–date business keys.
- No invalid dates detected.
- No negative units sold, inventory levels, order quantities, or demand forecasts.
- Positive unit costs and prices.
- Binary values for the promotion and stockout flags.
- No records where unit price was below unit cost.

The raw dataset was preserved, and a cleaned CSV was generated for analysis. The cleaned data was loaded into SQLite, and the key analytical metrics were checked independently using Python and SQL.

## 5. Limitations

1. **Simulated data:** The results describe the supplied dataset and should not be treated as evidence of real-world company performance.
2. **Unspecified currency:** The dataset does not establish a currency for financial values.
3. **Stockout measurement:** The stockout flag is zero for every record. A meaningful stockout rate cannot be measured from this field.
4. **Forecast evaluation:** The forecast metrics have not established superiority over a baseline forecasting method.
5. **Sales versus true demand:** Units sold may not represent true demand if product availability constrained sales.
6. **Historical aggregation:** Average inventory and reorder-point comparisons do not identify every SKU that may require replenishment at a particular moment.
7. **Time coverage:** The records cover January 1 through December 30, 2024; December 31 is not present.

## 6. Recommended Next Steps

- Compare the supplied demand forecasts with a simple baseline.
- Evaluate forecast errors by SKU, warehouse, and time period.
- Investigate records at or below reorder points at the SKU–warehouse level.
- Use a dataset with meaningful stockout events and confirmed inventory movements for stockout analysis.
- Test the analysis on real operational data before making business recommendations.

## 7. Conclusion

Inventory Intelligence demonstrates an end-to-end analytics workflow involving data cleaning, SQL-based analysis, KPI calculation, independent metric validation, and Power BI visualization.

The project identifies descriptive patterns in revenue, inventory thresholds, product contribution, and forecast behavior. Its reproducible workflow and explicit limitations make the results easier to verify and interpret.

The findings are analytical observations from the supplied dataset, not proof of improved business performance or reduced stockouts.
